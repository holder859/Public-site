A short history of the line

The Midwest Electric Railway began as a volunteer effort to save cars that streetcar and interurban companies across the region were sending to the scrapper. What started with a single flatcar and a few hundred feet of borrowed track has grown into a working railway, rebuilt piece by piece on original narrow-gauge right-of-way.

Every car in the fleet earned its place here the hard way. Some, like Car 381, spent decades as a fixed display before crews returned it to running condition. Others, like the wooden line car 3279, survive specifically because they were too useful to scrap — line cars strung and maintained the overhead wire that made the whole system possible.

The railway operates on Threshers show grounds during scheduled events, when the car barn crew brings cars out, tests them on the loop, and runs public rides on the same rail the original interurban lines once used.

Plan your visit

  • Where
    Threshers show grounds — Trolley Barn & Midwest Central Depot. See the park map for the trolley loop, marked in red, and boarding stops at the Depot and Log Village.
  • When
    Cars run during scheduled Threshers events. Check the board above before you come out — a car listed "in service" is confirmed running that day.
  • Boarding
    Board at the Midwest Central Depot or the Trolley Barn stop. Cars loop out toward Log Village and back.
  • Crew
    The railway is entirely volunteer-run — from motormen to the line car crew stringing wire. Ask a crew member about any car on the roster. Trolley barn tours avaliable whenever there is a crew member onsite.
